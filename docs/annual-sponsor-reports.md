# Annual sponsor reports

Open **Admin → Annual sponsor reports**, or `/admin/reports/annual?year=2026&recipient=NYDIG`. HRF has its own draft at the same URL with `recipient=HRF`. Event reports remain available at `/{conf}/admin/report`.

## Preparing an edition

1. Open the year and recipient. Database figures load immediately. The live draft automatically refreshes linked/imported X and YouTube recordings and X post analytics; no save, approval or reload is needed to collect them. Completed events, reviewed full-day broadcasts, and all @btcplusplus posts published in the report year count by default. Only global admins can access reports, saved editions, or Markdown previews.
2. Keep writing while collection runs. The status panel shows progress and platform failures; a failed fetch retains the previous snapshot and its collection date. Automatic recording checks and successful post snapshots are reused for six hours. **Refresh now** bypasses that cache when a retry is needed. Printing and freezing are enabled when collection finishes.
3. In **Edit report**, uncheck incorrect events or check **Exclude from this report** beside recordings/posts, then save your choices. Exclusions persist across source refreshes and remain available to undo. New completed events enter automatically within the cutoff; earlier saved event selections retain their exclusions. Per-event X decisions still control event reports; annual account-wide reach has its own post exclusions. NYDIG and HRF have independent annual selections.
4. Adjust the cutoff or hackathon coverage if necessary. Event start years and completion dates use the conference timezone. Future events are excluded from event totals; account-wide post impressions can include their promotion. Available hackathon records count automatically; incomplete historical records can be excluded without excluding an event's other statistics. Vienna and Vegas 2026 default to no hackathon; Floripa 2026 retains its incomplete-history setting.
5. Edit the letter, technical themes, community stories, expansion lessons, and next-year plans. Markdown previews update as you type. Automatic collection preserves unsaved narrative edits. If you change scope, exclusions or manual figures during collection, save those edits to render the latest figures with your choices.
6. Annual YouTube/X exports remain optional, with account/source, reporting period, export date and coverage. A collected annual X post library takes precedence over a manual impressions total, and annual post exclusions reduce the headline. Manual X impressions are a fallback only when no library exists. Overlapping exports are not summed automatically.
7. **Save & freeze edition** preserves the collected figures, source choices and text together. Frozen editions never collect new data. Print the frozen edition to PDF using A4, 100% scale, background graphics enabled, and browser headers/footers disabled to preserve the exact appearance sent to the sponsor.

The sponsor-focused edition keeps the existing page structure, with charts, event artwork, and people taking priority over copy. Event recaps use short summaries and larger counters; winner cards show the team, prize, and one build description. Challenges, documented follow-ups, and sources remain in the editor. Printed pages omit collection footnotes and explanatory chart captions; chart scope remains available in accessible labels and hover text. When lessons or next-year plans are present, they receive a dedicated follow-up page before the closing thank-you; blank text and editorial prompts do not add a page. Page counts and folios include this page. Untouched old default copy upgrades in live drafts; custom writing, intentional blanks, and frozen narrative remain intact.

The editor includes contribution details, a spending breakdown, and a participant quote. These stay out of the report while they contain only drafting prompts. Supply the actual funded activity, finance records, and a quote cleared for publication before including them. Project stories start from public submissions; verified follow-ups include source links and can be edited or cleared. Missing follow-ups are omitted. Saving preserves each project's story fields for the frozen edition.

For NYDIG’s 2026 edition, the organizer confirmed that headline sponsorship funded hackathons, crew travel, catering, event space, and professional video recordings. The letter and a short funding-page caption use those confirmed activities. No spending amounts or allocation percentages have been supplied.

The 2026 report includes three quotes supplied by the organizer: feedback about bringing people together on the community page, the quality of the recordings on the reach page, and keeping the Bitcoin community alive on the closing page. The community and closing quotes include their confirmed authors; the recording quote remains unattributed. Each quote can be edited or removed in the narrative editor.

## Definitions and limits

- The cutoff selects events, not a historical database snapshot. Figures reflect records at collection time. All annual event metrics and deduplicated totals are read in a single repeatable-read transaction.
- The year-over-year chart compares published gatherings completed through the same local calendar date in each year. February 29 maps to February 28 in a non-leap comparison year. It hides when prior-year records are absent or an eligible current-year event is excluded. This compares event counts, not registration or prize totals with unequal historical coverage.
- The community repeat figure counts distinct people registered for more than one included event in the report year. It does not assert a check-in or a return from a previous year. Older frozen attendance editions retain their check-in basis. Empty figures are omitted.
- Sponsor attribution includes only the recipient's paid or committed, non-archived sponsorships attached to included events. Multiple sponsorship records count an event once. The headline-sponsor description requires a headline sponsorship at every counted event; this does not claim that all funding has settled or assign spending to a specific use.
- Registration bars count distinct registered people per event, including paid and complimentary registrations without requiring a check-in. Revoked registrations are excluded, while valid historical registrations survive account deletion. Annual identities are deduplicated by person, falling back to email. The donut compares registration at one included event against multiple events; it does not claim first-ever participation, payment, or attendance. Older frozen editions retain their original check-in basis.
- Event recap circles omit missing statistics, incomplete/no-hackathon prizes, and empty program counts. Measured zero recording views remain a valid statistic. Long values retain their precision in compact type.
- Community and funding pages chart cumulative registrations, offered prizes, submitted projects, and hackathon participations across the report year. Steps occur on each included event's local end date, with calendar-spaced dates and a zero baseline. They use the same totals and coverage exclusions as the headlines; these are event aggregates, not historical daily signup or payment measurements. With fewer than two distinct dated observations, or missing dates, the report retains the headline instead of drawing a partial series. Older frozen editions keep their original attendance and prize basis.
- X post impressions are lifetime counters on all available @btcplusplus posts published during the report year, collected on the displayed date. They are independent of event selection and the event cutoff; they are not impressions earned only in that year. Frozen editions retain their original account-wide or event-based snapshot.
- Program counts are accepted, non-archived scheduled entries, deduplicated across multiple recordings. They do not claim confirmed delivery. Speakers are distinct profile identities across programs.
- Projects are submitted/advanced entries, not necessarily distinct teams. Hackathon participations sum each included event's distinct project-member count where hackathon records are available. A person participating in multiple events counts once per event; multiple projects within one event count once. Excluded events and incomplete hackathon records do not contribute. Winning projects require finalized results. Sats offered in prizes sum numeric sat prize amounts multiplied by the award’s configured number of winning projects. Available, unawarded and awarded prizes count regardless of assignments, result finalization or payout status. Draft/archived awards, non-sats prizes, unresolved pools and invalid amounts are excluded. Existing frozen editions retain their original awarded or sent/claimed basis; new editions use offered prizes. Individual winner cards still describe the prizes awarded to that winner.
- The community gallery selects up to 12 existing profile portraits across included events, aiming for a mix of speakers and winning-project builders. Within each event, speakers who participated in an accepted, non-archived panel take priority over other speakers; events without usable panel portraits fall back to the remaining program speakers. Panel participation is saved with portraits in new frozen editions. It skips missing/default images and repeated identities or photos. Event recaps show up to three program speakers; winner cards place team portraits before project artwork. New frozen snapshots retain the speaker portraits they collected. Editions without available portraits fall back to the city map.
- The project showcase includes all finalized overall first-place projects from included events with available hackathon records, including ties. Sponsor challenge awards and honorable mentions do not qualify on their own. When builder or resource-use copy is present, the first winner page holds two projects alongside that narrative. Subsequent pages hold up to four projects; a page with three winners uses full-width rows with event names and circular team portraits beside the project details. Additional winners continue onto new pages with updated folios. Missing historical results require source records before a winner can be shown.
- A frozen edition stores text and aggregate/event evidence, never attendee emails, and clears the CSRF token. It does not pin CSS/fonts or external project destinations; retain the exported PDF for exact visual archival.
- No new migration is required beyond `111_sponsor_reports.sql`. Annual drafts use `annual-v1/<year>/<recipient>` and immutable editions append `/frozen/<uuid>`. Draft updates use optimistic revision checks, and draft-save plus snapshot-insert are atomic.

Add `?recap-regression=1` to check Berlin’s 21.75M prize value, missing-stat circles, and account-wide reach. It does not seed or alter application data.

For portrait layout checks, optionally set `ANNUAL_REPORT_PEOPLE_PREVIEW` to a local JSON file keyed by event tag, with `SpeakerPortraits` and `WinningProjects` in the annual snapshot format. Use public profile/project data with its original names and event associations; the preview's aggregate metrics remain illustrative.

The organizer-confirmed 2026 community quote is attributed to Godefroid Chapelle (Gotcha), and the closing quote to Rachel Rybarczyk. Live drafts load their name, avatar, organization, and X handle from their fixed person IDs. An empty profile organization falls back to recorded speaker affiliations. Deleted profiles are omitted. Attributions are attached to the confirmed wording, so replacing a quote does not credit the replacement to the original author. New frozen editions retain the attribution fields; older frozen editions are unchanged. For local layout checks, `ANNUAL_REPORT_QUOTE_PREVIEW` accepts a JSON map of quote section keys to `annualQuoteAuthor` records, including the confirmed `Quote` text.

These routes are read-only, opt-in local fixtures; the concept is not added to published or frozen reports.

## Event location globes

Event highlights include pale, right-aligned globe SVGs with the host country highlighted and a city marker. The final one-event page uses a full-width artwork panel and a centered speaker row. Unknown event tags omit the globe. The map data is [Natural Earth via world-atlas 2.0.2](https://github.com/topojson/world-atlas), projected with [D3's orthographic projection](https://d3js.org/d3-geo/azimuthal#geoOrthographic); no mapping service or browser JavaScript is needed. To regenerate the assets:

```sh
curl -fL https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/world-atlas@2.0.2/countries-110m.json -o /tmp/report-countries.json
curl -fL https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/d3@7.9.0/dist/d3.min.js -o /tmp/report-d3.cjs
curl -fL https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/topojson-client@3.1.0/dist/topojson-client.min.js -o /tmp/report-topojson.cjs
node ops/generate-report-globes.cjs /tmp/report-countries.json /tmp/report-d3.cjs /tmp/report-topojson.cjs
```

## PDF layout verification

Use the report’s **Print / Save as PDF** button. It prepares an isolated snapshot at A4 width, waits for fonts and images, and checks every designed page against the printable page size. Oversized content stops export and names the affected pages; shorten the copy or adjust pagination before retrying. Failed assets also stop export. The checked snapshot is what opens in the print dialog. Browser menu/keyboard printing bypasses this check. Choose A4, 100% scale, background graphics on, and browser headers/footers off; custom print-dialog settings can still change pagination.

The A4 print layout reserves space for saved narrative text, keeps three reach headlines in one row, and uses content-sized event recap rows. It never clips or truncates prose to enforce a page count. The funding overview contains the charts, event cards and short sponsorship caption; longer builder and resource-use narratives appear with the winning projects. No saved copy is shortened.

For regression review with an existing report, set `ANNUAL_REPORT_COPY_PREVIEW` to a local JSON object of narrative/event-highlight keys and text. It is loaded only by the opt-in preview harness; it never updates saved drafts. Export the preview with print backgrounds and CSS page size, then inspect the PDF page count, folios, footer placement, and rendered pages. The November 2026 NYDIG regression uses a roughly 300-word letter, 300 words of follow-up copy, 70-word event recaps, three reach headlines, five hackathon cards, and full city/country names in the community links.
